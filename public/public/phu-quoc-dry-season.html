Why January Stands Out for an Island Break

Among the dry months, January is the one the guide singles out as the sweet spot. It brings together several conditions that rarely line up so neatly on a tropical island.

Driest weather

Rainfall in January is typically under 30 mm for the whole month. Across December to March the monthly total stays below 50 mm, but January sits at the low end of that range, so outdoor plans rarely need a backup.

Cooler temperatures

Daytime temperatures in January run around 24 to 29°C, a little cooler than the hotter shoulder months. Walking around markets, visiting the north of the island or taking a long boat trip feels comfortable rather than draining.

Calm seas

Sea conditions are calm, with small waves. This favours island-hopping tours in the south, snorkelling over coral and swimming at sheltered beaches such as Bai Sao or Bai Khem.

After the holiday rush

Minh hoạ: phu quoc dry season

January follows the Christmas and New Year peak. Demand eases slightly once the festive period ends, which can make the island feel a little more relaxed in the middle of the month. Tet can fall in late January or February, though, so check the lunar calendar before fixing dates.

When to book

The guide advises booking January trips by around August. Accommodation for festive periods is often reserved six to nine months ahead.

A simple January plan

Mornings on the water, afternoons resting in the shade, and evenings at a night market or watching the sunset on the west coast make good use of the season.

Conclusion
