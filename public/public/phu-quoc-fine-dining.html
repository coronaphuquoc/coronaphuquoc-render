Ordering a Cantonese Style Feast at Bach Giai

Bach Giai sits inside the Almaz Culinary Center in the Bai Dai area of northern Phu Quoc, and it is the place on the island many visitors pick when they want classic Chinese cooking served with full table service. Ordering well here is mostly about understanding how a Chinese banquet is built.

The dishes to build around

The menu leans on traditional Chinese roasting and slow cooking. The landing names several signature plates: roasted chicken, crispy roasted pork, Peking duck, a BBQ Four Treasures platter and an abalone soup that regular diners rate highly.

  • Start the table with the abalone soup while the roasted dishes are prepared.
  • Choose one roasted centrepiece, either Peking duck or the crispy pork, rather than both for a small group.
  • Add the BBQ Four Treasures if you want a sampler of several meats on one plate.
  • Balance the meat with greens and rice so the meal does not feel heavy.

Sharing the table

Chinese meals are designed for sharing, so order for the table rather than one main per person. A group of four can comfortably share a soup, two roasted plates and a couple of vegetable dishes. Let the staff advise on portion sizes, since service here is attentive and they know how large each plate is.

Practical points

Opening hours are not printed on the landing page, which asks guests to contact the resort for current times. Ask about hours and seating when you book, especially for a weekend or a holiday evening.

Conclusion

Treat dinner at Bach Giai as a shared banquet: one soup, one roasted centrepiece, a sampler plate and something green. Further reading: island eating guide, noodles to banquets.
