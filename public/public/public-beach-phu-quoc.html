Reading the Seabed: Shallow and Deep Shores in Phu Quoc

Water depth decides more about a beach day than sand colour does. A parent with toddlers, a lap swimmer and a snorkeller each want a very different seabed, and Phu Quoc offers all three if you know where to look.

The long shallow shelf of Bai Dai

On the north-western coast, Bai Dai slopes so gently that the water stays around shin height for about 30 metres from the waterline. Currents are weak and the sand is fine and pale gold. For small children who want to splash rather than swim, this gradient is the main attraction, and dry-season visibility often reaches 8 to 10 metres.

Khem for proper swimming

Down in the south, Khem shelves slowly to chest depth roughly 40 metres out. That gives adults room to swim real strokes in clear, calm water, and the guide treats it as the island's benchmark for swimming quality. It stays settled through March and April as well.

Sheltered water at Sao

Minh hoạ: public beach phu quoc

Sao sits in a tight, protected crescent. Waves are rare except for the occasional south-easterly squall, and the colour moves from clear to emerald within a few metres. It suits kayaking and relaxed floating.

A quick depth cheat sheet

  • Toddlers and nervous swimmers: Bai Dai
  • Distance swimmers: Khem
  • Kayaks and calm floating: Sao
  • Snorkelling over reefs: the An Thoi islands by boat

Conclusion

Match the seabed to the people in your group before you choose a beach, and the day will mostly take care of itself. Further reading: nine island shores compared by activity.
