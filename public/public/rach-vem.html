Inside the Nha Be Floating Houses of Rach Vem

Look out from the shore at Rach Vem and you will see a cluster of wooden structures standing in the sea. Locals call them nha be, and they are the most distinctive sight in this northern fishing village of around 180 to 200 households.

How the houses are built

Each floating house sits on wooden stilts somewhere between 200 and 400 metres offshore. They are modest in size, roughly 25 to 30 square metres, with plank floors and metal roofs. There is nothing decorative about them. They are working spaces shaped by wind, salt and the needs of families who earn a living from the sea.

Homes and restaurants at once

The same structures serve two purposes. Fishing families live on them, and many also run them as floating seafood restaurants for visitors. Floating seafood farms just off the shoreline follow the same pattern: they feed tourists and also reflect the traditional livelihood of the community. When you eat out on the water here, you are sitting inside someone's working home.

Visiting with respect

  • Ask before photographing people at work or at home.
  • Keep voices low during the morning, when nets are being handled.
  • Take any rubbish back to shore with you.
  • Carry small cash, since floating restaurants are informal businesses.
  • Follow the boat operator's instructions when stepping on and off.

What you can learn there

Travellers can chat with fishermen, watch them cast nets and haul in fish, and sometimes join in to understand coastal life. A short conversation, even with gestures, often tells you more about the island than a full day of sightseeing.
