Reading the Martyrs Memorial at the Coconut Tree Prison

Many visitors walk through the memorial at the southern prison site quickly, take one photo and move on. The structure rewards a slower look, because almost every part of its shape carries meaning.

What the memorial is

The Phu Quoc Prisoned Martyrs Memorial was built in 2013 and covers 12,420 square meters. It honours more than 500 soldiers who died inside the prison because of torture and inhumane treatment, and their names are recorded here.

The shape and what it says

Two waves rise about five metres high on either side of a hollow central pyramid, which sinks roughly two metres into the ground. The design concept translates as "The People Who Emerged from That Place". The waves read as the hardship pressing in, and the figure of emergence speaks to survivors and to those who did not leave.

  • Stand at a distance first to see the waves as a pair.
  • Walk to the centre to feel the depth of the pyramid.
  • Read the names slowly rather than scanning them.

Paying respects

Visitors are welcome to offer incense at the central monument, and incense is available on site. Staff ask for a quiet atmosphere throughout the memorial area, and flash photography or filming may be discouraged near it, so check the posted signs.

Where it sits in your route

Most people reach the memorial after Zone B2, which is the most intense part of the complex. Coming from the reconstructions to this open, deliberate space helps the visit shift from shock to remembrance. A similar theme returns at Doi Sim, where a statue about five metres tall shows a person rising between two waves.
