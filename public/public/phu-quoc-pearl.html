Akoya, South Sea, Tahitian and Mabe Explained Simply

Walk into any showroom on Phu Quoc and you will hear four names repeated: Akoya, South Sea, Tahitian and Mabe. Each comes from a different oyster or method, and knowing the difference makes the grading table far less confusing.

Akoya

These are small, round and very lustrous. They are the pearls behind most classic strands and simple stud earrings, and they usually come in white or cream tones. If you picture a traditional pearl necklace, you are picturing Akoya.

South Sea

South Sea pearls are large and creamy white, sometimes with a golden cast. The landing page describes them as the most valuable of the four types produced on the island, which is worth remembering when a price tag surprises you.

Tahitian

These dark, iridescent pearls come from black-lipped oysters. Their colour can read as grey, green or deep aubergine depending on the light. Quoc An farm, near the Suoi May intersection, is known for its black Tahitian collection.

Mabe

Mabe pearls are hemispherical because they grow against the inside wall of the shell rather than around a loose bead. Their flat back makes them a natural fit for earrings and pendants.

  • Ask the guide to place one of each side by side under the same light.
  • Note which type you like before you look at price labels.
  • Request a certificate naming the type once you decide.

Conclusion

With the four names straight in your head, the showroom becomes a place to compare rather than guess. Further reading: pearl farm visits, types and prices.
